Weightmark

Privacy policy

Publisher: Gowtham Reddy Ambala · Contact: support@gwtmlabs.com · Effective November 12, 2026

What the app stores

Weightmark stores what you enter on your iPhone: your weight entries, their dates and notes; your food entries (the food, amount, meal, date, and its calories and nutrients); your profile (name, height, date of birth, goal weight, sex, activity level, pace, units); and your appearance and reminder settings. These power your history, graph, streak, goal, BMI, daily food totals, estimated daily burn and calorie target.

The calorie estimate and target are calculated on your iPhone from your profile, food log and weight trend. Nothing about them is sent anywhere. Sex and activity level are optional and used only for this estimate.

Weightmark has no account. It does not send any of this to the developer or to any server, and it has no advertising, tracking or analytics. The only data that leaves your iPhone is a barcode you look up, described below.

Food logging

Search. Typing a food name searches a food database built into the app, based on USDA FoodData Central. The search runs on your iPhone and is not sent anywhere.

Barcodes. When you scan a barcode or look one up, Weightmark sends that barcode number to Open Food Facts, a public food database, to get the product's name and nutrition. The request identifies the app and its version. Like any web request, it also shows Open Food Facts your IP address. Nothing else is sent: no name, profile, weight, food log or Health data. Open Food Facts handles its server logs under its own privacy policy. Products you've looked up are saved on your iPhone, so scanning the same barcode again doesn't contact Open Food Facts.

Camera. The camera is used only while the scanner is open, to read barcodes. Photos and video are not saved or sent.

Food entries keep the nutrition values from when you logged them. Product data from Open Food Facts is available under the Open Database License.

Apple Health

Apple Health is off until you connect it in Profile. Before iOS asks for permission, the app explains what connecting does. When connected, Weightmark:

  • Reads your body weight from Health, and nothing else. It adds the first reading of a day as that day's weigh-in, only for days that have none. iOS may open the app in the background to do this when Health receives a new weight.
  • Saves the weigh-ins you type in Weightmark to Health as your body weight, if you allow it. Editing or deleting such a weigh-in updates or removes the copy Weightmark saved. It cannot change data saved by other apps or devices.

Food entries are not saved to Apple Health, and Weightmark does not read food data from Health.

Weights read from Health are stored on your iPhone with your other weigh-ins. They are not sent to the developer or anyone else, are not sold, and are not used for advertising or marketing. You can disconnect in Profile at any time; weigh-ins already added stay. You control the permissions themselves in Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices > Weightmark.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Weightmark can remind you on a day you haven't logged, and tell you when weigh-ins are added from Apple Health. The reminder is off until you turn it on in Profile. All of them are created on your iPhone; no notification service or server is involved.

Export and import

Export makes a CSV file of your weigh-ins on your iPhone and opens the share sheet. Where that file goes is your choice, and Weightmark does not keep track of it. Import reads a CSV file you pick and adds its weigh-ins for days that have none.

Storage and backups

Your records live in the app's local storage on your iPhone; food entries and looked-up products are in a separate database there. Weightmark has no cloud sync or backup service of its own. Your iPhone's own backups (for example iCloud Backup) may include the app's data and restore it with your device; Apple and your device settings control those backups. If Apple Health is connected, the weigh-ins you type are also saved to Health, which keeps them under Apple's own Health data protections.

The app does not add its own encryption on top of your iPhone's. Use your iPhone's passcode and security features to protect it.

Your choices

You can edit your profile, and edit or delete any weight entry in History. “Delete all weigh-ins” at the bottom of Profile removes every weigh-in from the iPhone after you confirm, and disconnects Apple Health; it does not change what is in Health. It does not remove food entries.

You can change or delete any food entry on the Log tab. A deleted food entry disappears from your log and totals, but a record marked as deleted stays in the app's storage on your iPhone until you delete the app. Deleting the app removes its data from that iPhone; earlier device backups may still hold a copy. Because nothing is sent to the developer, lost records cannot be recovered by the developer.

If you email support

If you write to support@gwtmlabs.com, the developer, Cloudflare (which forwards the message) and Google (which provides Gmail) receive your email address and whatever you include. Support emails are kept only as long as needed to help you, and you can ask for yours to be deleted. Please don't send your weight history or other sensitive details unless they're needed.

Health information

Weightmark is a personal tracking tool. Its calorie estimate and target are not medical advice and do not apply during pregnancy or breastfeeding. If you have or have had an eating disorder, talk to a doctor before following a calorie target. Its BMI information is general screening information; it does not diagnose any condition or provide treatment. Food nutrition values come from public databases and product labels and may be incomplete or wrong. Talk to a qualified healthcare professional about health decisions.

Changes

If the app's data practices change, this page will be updated along with its effective date.
